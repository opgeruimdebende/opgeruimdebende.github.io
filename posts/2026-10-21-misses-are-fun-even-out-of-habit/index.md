---
title: Misses are fun, even out of habit
date: 2026-10-21
summary: A miss in Klaar! is built to feel good, even when you miss over and over again.
---

When you let go of a rectangle that adds up to nothing, the numbers bounce back a little. Now that bounce has a sound too.

I thought about Go players. Out of habit, they tap a stone on the edge of the table. It is not part of the game, it is just something to do with your hands. As long as the feedback is pleasing, I think some players will do the same here: miss on purpose, quickly and deliberately, just to feel it. So the test for this sound was not one tap. It was a habit. If a short, muted, soft thud can keep up with that and still feel good, then it is well designed.

That is the whole design. It is very short, with almost no tail, so one thud is gone before the next one arrives. It sits low. The first version was a bit high, so it was nudged slightly lower, and the highest takes were dropped. And there are four slightly different takes, played in turn, so a fast run of misses does not sound like one sound copied again and again. At most three can sound at once.

Habit taps plays sixteen misses, a tenth of a second apart. Try one take to hear what the four are for. In one of my own Wa games before it had any sounds at all, there is a stretch of 11 misses in just over three seconds.
