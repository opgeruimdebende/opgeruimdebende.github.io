Klaar! is getting its first sounds, and early on I decided on a percussion mallet for its voice. I like its warmth, and its modesty. A mallet can still be expressive and celebratory when it needs to be, it just does not shout about it. That fits the game.

Claude made a single mallet note in code. Every musical sound in the game is that same note, played higher or lower, always from the same small scale of five notes per octave. The climb in Hap when you clear several in a row. The two notes of a hint. The two falling notes of No more moves. The chord when the board is cleared.

My favourite is what happens right after Klaar! pops in. Nine little tokens, 1 to 9, burst out and tidy themselves into a row. Each one now plays a note as it lands, 1 the lowest and 9 the highest, so you can hear the row getting in order. Wa's celebration is shorter and modest compared to Hap's, which is rarer and gets to be more expressive.

We are building with music in mind, which we don't have yet. For Hap I want something like the music of the original apple-box game. It may not be the world's best composition, but it suits the game so well that playing with that song became part of the hype. So maybe it is one of the best puzzle game tunes. Wa will need its own, and I don't know yet what it should be. Not generic lo-fi lounge, that much I know.

Once there is a song, the scale can follow its key. Then the Klaar! chord will sound like a small improvisation on whatever song it just interrupted, instead of something dropped on top. This is just an idea for now since we haven't started working on the game's music.
