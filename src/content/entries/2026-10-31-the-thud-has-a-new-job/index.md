Ten days ago I wrote about the thud a miss makes, and I hoped it would survive a week of fidgeting. It did, just not as a miss.

Once every clear started playing a note of its own tune, the thud began to stand out. It had been a nice novelty, but next to melodies it stood out too much. We tried something else: a mallet note damped the moment it is struck, tuned to the chord that is playing. It is soft, it still sounds like a miss, and it sat better with the music.

I was still attached to the low, wooden thud from before. So we gave it a different role: now the wooden thud keeps time as the rhythmic heartbeat for Hap. The first versions were too mechanical, and I didn't want to stress people out with a ticking clock. We played around with some simple downbeats, and opted for a low thud on one and three and a dry wooden snap on two and four, with a little pickup every other bar:

Hap's beat runs at 128 beats per minute, and your notes play over it. If a clear lands close to the beat, within 35 milliseconds, its note waits for it, like the window for a perfect hit in a rhythm game. Anything further off plays the moment you clear. That is never a mistake, it is your deliberate timing.

Eventually, the wood came back in Wa as well, low and tuned, returning as better tuned misses. And if you find the underlying beat distracting, it has its own switch in Settings, called Rhythm, to turn on and off.
