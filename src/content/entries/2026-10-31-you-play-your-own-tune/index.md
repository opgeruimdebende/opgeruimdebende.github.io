Ten days ago I wrote that we hadn't started on Klaar!'s music. The plan back then was to compose a hook on my guitar and turn it into a loop for each mode. That plan has changed. Now, I don't write Klaar!'s music. You do.

Every clear plays one mallet note, and which note depends on how you are playing. A quick run stays close, in small steps. A long think reaches for a more colourful note, and your next clear brings it home. After a long break, the tune starts fresh. It listens to how you play, not to what a better move would have been, so it never gives anything away and never tells you off.

Each mode has its own voice. Wa is in F major and starts on a minor chord, so a game can sound as if it began mid-tune. Hap is in D major, and brighter. Both rest on a familiar chord progression with one or two small surprises on top. I took inspiration from Jacob Collier, who eases an audience in with something familiar before he brings the unexpected.

Only a cleared board resolves the tune. That is our thank-you. Wa ends warm and safe; Hap ends a little open, like the opening of the next game. Leave a board unfinished and you get one soft note, left hanging. The board is still there whenever you want to come back to it.

I prototyped this idea with Claude, and the first rough versions reminded me of learning the piano as a kid, when I was encouraged to just finish the song, however many wrong notes I hit on the way. It wasn't skilled or refined, but it was music, especially when you made it to the end.

We call it Footnotes. The board already keeps your footprints; now your game leaves footnotes too. And because the tune comes from your moves alone, a finished game always repeats your unique tune. Your play writes the sheet music, and Klaar! is the instrument.
