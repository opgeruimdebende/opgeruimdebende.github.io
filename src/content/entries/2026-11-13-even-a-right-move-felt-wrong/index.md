When friends started playing the early build, the music kicking in was one of the things that surprised them most. That made me really happy. Then one of my Korean friends said, "소리가 너무 땡땡거려서 맞춰도 틀린 것 같아요": the sound clangs so much that even a right move feels wrong. 땡 is also the sound a quiz show makes when you get the answer wrong. Fair point.

I never planned for Klaar! to be this musical. But I'm a musician, and once the novelty of "it works!" wore off, I could hear things to improve. I no longer see the music as secondary to the puzzle. It's what completes the experience. So I moved it up the list before the December launch and spent two days listening, reconstructing, and listening again.

That came to 36 listening rounds. Claude played my own games through the new rules, I listened and analyzed what I heard, and that went into the next round. We tried giving every game a recurring theme; it didn't work, so it went. Most rounds ended with something taken out. The rule I kept coming back to: if something new comes in, something old has to go.

Two things changed that you'll hear. The mallet is softer, a felt head instead of a hard one. In a blind test I heard the felt one as the clearer strike every time. And the music now has two homes, a bright one and a shaded one. When you've spent some time pondering over a board and finally find the move, that clear turns the music to its other home. Klaar! acknowledges your breakthrough: the music's biggest change lands on the most satisfying moment of play.

I wonder if people will experience the music as I designed it. I'm the worst person to judge, because I'm very attached to it. Playtests will reveal its impact and what's left to close. For now, I'm satisfied we made good progress, and I'm looking forward to hearing it in other people's games.
